## Sim flow (M03)

Intent and turn flow, in the order a click travels:

1. UI or AI builds an `IntentBody` (`{kind, seat, ...}`, no id) and calls `host.submitIntent(body)`. The host copies it (`copyBody`), hands out the id (`IdAllocator`, one counter per seat) and files it under turn `scheduledTurn(host tick)` = T + 2. Nothing is validated here: garbage becomes an intent that `step` refuses.
2. The caller reports elapsed real time with `host.advance(dtMs)`. The `FixedStepAccumulator` turns it into whole ticks (10 Hz at 1x; speeds 0, 1, 2, 4; at most 8 ticks per call; the host reads no clock).
3. For every tick the host calls `step(state, {turn: state.tick, intents})`. `step` validates each intent (`validateIntent`; an invalid one is a no-op that raises `state.rejects`), sorts the valid ones by (seat, id, kind, payload), refuses duplicates of (seat, id), applies them (`applyIntent`: a Spawn that is legal in shape but not in the state, for example on sea or by a seat that has spawned, and an Attack without land, border or troops are refused here with their reason), runs the conquest (every attack gains progress and takes its cells, D82), the enclave pass of the bucket `tick % 20` (D86) and the economy tick, draws once from `state.rng` and increments `state.tick`. A turn that does not equal `state.tick`, is not a list or holds more than 4096 intents is an error RESULT and changes nothing.
4. The host drains `state.outbox` after each step (render-only events; not hashed, not serialised) and sums the paints of the frame into ONE `diff` event (`fromTick`, `toTick`, cells with their new ownerId, reject count; if the outbox dropped events in the frame, `cells`/`owners` are empty and `fullOwner` holds the whole owner array, which the consumer must adopt, D69); every `hashEveryTicks` it also emits a `hash` event. Each listener receives its own deep copy.
5. `host.requestSnapshot()` resolves with `{tick, hash, bytes, resume}` where `bytes = serialize(state)` and `resume` holds the host's id counters and scheduled turns; `deserialize(bytes)` gives the state back (or an error result), `MainThreadHost.resume(snapshot)` a host that continues exactly like the straight run (D68).

### Snapshot growth (M05)

What crosses the host seam, and what each part costs (all of it plain typed arrays, copied per listener, transferred over a Worker):
- `diff` event, once per `advance` that ran ticks: the changed cells and their owners (or `fullOwner`, 1 MB, after an outbox overflow) plus `diff.view`, a `ViewSnapshot` with the tick, the reject counter and the last refusal (reason, tick), the match facts, the packed per-seat statistics (8 fields x seats u32 = 832 bytes for 26 seats) and `attacks` (the attacks running as pairs of owner ids, a few dozen bytes, D143); `owner` and `terrain` are null here. The mirror applies it in place: no allocation, no copy of the owner array per frame.
- `requestView()`: the same `ViewSnapshot` plus FULL copies of the owner array (1 MB) and the terrain layer (0.5 MB); the page asks once at start, `ViewMirror.attach` then follows the diffs.
- `requestSnapshot()`: the whole serialised state (about 5 MB) for saving, resuming and hashing; never per frame.
A new per-seat statistic is a new block of `STAT_FIELDS` (and a new `SimView` accessor); anything bigger than a per-seat number belongs in its own request, not in the per-frame diff. The ownership TEXTURE (2 MB RGBA8) is not a host snapshot: the page keeps its own CPU copy current from the diffs and uploads it whole at most 10 times per second of fx time.

### The AI call (M05 part C)

The AI runs in the sim's own thread, never in the UI. `MainThreadHost.runTicks` does, for every tick: (1) `driverView.refresh()` (a `StateView` over the live state: the per-seat statistics are recomputed, 3 us), (2) `driver.onTick(view, submit)`, (3) `step(state, {turn, intents})` with the intents filed for this turn. `AiDriver.onTick` calls `think(view, memory, rng)` for every AI seat (seats 1 to 25) with that seat's `AiMemory` and the driver's own stream; a thought returns `[]` on nine ticks in ten, and what it returns is handed to `submit`, i.e. `host.submitIntent`, which files it under the turn T + 2 like any intent (copied, given an id per seat, validated by `step`). So the AI sees the state at the end of tick T - 1, its intent runs in the step of tick T + 2, and a refusal by the sim (an attack whose free land was taken in between) is counted in `state.rejects` like any other. The AI reads: `view.tick`, `phase`, `stats.*` per seat, `owner` and `terrainClass` (only for placing a capital), `contactLength(a, b)`, and nothing else. The page's `ViewMirror` is untouched by the AI: it follows the host's diff events like for any seat. M09 replaces the host option by the Worker-side driver of `src/host/aiDriver.ts` unchanged; the AI memory and stream are NOT yet in `HostResume` (BACKLOG).

Over a Worker (M24a) the same calls become the messages of `src/host/protocol.ts` (`submit`, `speed`, `advance`, `snapshot`, `dispose` one way; `event`, `snapshot`, `error` the other); `serveHost` runs in the worker, `RemoteHost` is the `SimHost` the page holds. Nothing in a message is a class instance, a function or a reference into the sim.

Determinism rules that hold for every file in `src/sim`: integers only (a single, labelled float kernel: `src/sim/canary.ts`); only `Math.floor`, `Math.max`, `Math.sqrt`, `Math.imul` and the other exactly rounded members; no `Map`, `Set`, `JSON`, promises or `.sort()` without a comparator; multi-byte values are written through `DataView` with an explicit byte order and hashed through shifts; the generator state is inside the state. The golden hashes in `e2e/goldens/sim-hashes.json` pin all of it.

## Rules for layers that sit on the terrain (M07a audit; read before M08, M10, M12/M13a, M15a)

1. Precision of props. The terrain material places a vertex relative to the eye (`uCamHi` / `uCamLo`, the chunk's centre as a high and a low part), so it is exact to millimetres at 300 m above the ground. A prop placed from absolute float32 coordinates (an `InstancedMesh` whose `instanceMatrix` holds positions near the 6.4e6 m planet radius, a helicopter, a shadow blob, dust, a missile) has a float32 ulp of 0.5 m there and would swim against the ground as the camera moves. The rule for every such layer: keep the position in float64 on the CPU as an ORIGIN (the centre of the region the layer covers, re-based when the camera has moved more than about 20 km from it) and store only the OFFSETS from that origin in the instance matrices (under 100 km: millimetres). The mesh's own `position` is the origin minus the camera, formed in float64 on the CPU each frame (or the origin is passed as a high / low pair like `uCamHi` / `uCamLo`, with the same two-part subtraction in the vertex shader). A layer that needs a GLSL snippet for it adds it to `src/render/shaders/common.ts` once. Test: the jitter test of `e2e/terrain.test.ts` (a camera shift of 1e-7 degrees changes under 0.2 % of the pixels) is repeated with the layer switched on.
2. Height of things above the ground. The renderer displaces the ground by `HEIGHT_EXAGGERATION` (12) times the 10 km raster; the simulation's altitudes (game metres: `EXAG_sim * meanGround(cell) + AGL`, or occluder + clearance, from `src/sim/rules.ts`) are another scale (`GAMEPLAY_HEIGHT_EXAG`, M11a). A prop drawn at the sim altitude would sit inside the visible mountains (Himalaya: about 40 km against a displaced ground at 60 km). The rule: keep the height ABOVE the ground and re-base it on the visible ground: `renderRadius(x, y) = groundRadius(x, y) + (altGame - EXAG_sim * meanSim(cell))` where `groundRadius` is `HeightField.surfaceRadius` (the displaced ground, also under picking) and the bracket is the sim's height above its own ground in game metres, scaled to render units. M08's `transforms.ts` (later) states this once; M12a/M13a call it. Test: a helicopter instance is at least the clearance above `groundHeightAt` along a real Alps route, and its horizontal position equals the sim's within 0.5 cell.
3. Budgets. Row 0 of "Budgetar" is pinned (150 000 triangles, 30 draw calls) by `tests/budgets.test.ts`. The terrain, the shell and the stars use 3 draw calls and at most 96 000 + 9 024 triangles in the test tier, which leaves about 45 000 triangles and 27 draw calls for every later layer together. M10 (structures, first layer after M07a) is the milestone that changes row 0: it raises the draw-call limit to 100 (the M21 target) in the test file, the table and DECISIONS in one commit, gives each new row its own allowance (structures at most 15 000 triangles), and, if the layers need more than the 45 000 triangles, lowers the test tier's `chunkMax` from 150 to 100 (64 000 terrain triangles) in the same commit and regenerates the contact-sheet goldens once. Nobody else edits row 0.

## Chunk-terräng mot det fasta nätet (M07a, `renderer.info`, hela scenen inklusive atmosfärskalets 9 024 trianglar)

Samma fragmentskuggare (pixelarbetet per bildpunkt är detsamma; chunkvarianten lägger bara till en `clamp` för fasetternas vikt). Trianglar och anrop mäts med `renderer.info` i headless Chromium (`?test=1&chunktier=...`, så att varje nivås chunktäthet körs med testlägets fasta inställningar); det fasta nätets tal är nätets `2 x kolumner x rader` plus skalet. Standardnivån är `medium` (M05 gav användaren ett stadigt 30 fps där).

Det fasta nätet kostar lika mycket i varje vy; chunk-trädet kostar 0,41 gånger så mycket i standardvyn (medelnivån: 110 400 mot 271 168, bara den synliga halvan ritas och nivå 2 har exakt det gamla nätets 0,70 graders ruta), 6 till 8 procent mindre vid 3 200 och 6 000 km och 3 till 9 procent mer när kameran är nära (900 och 220 km). Taken per nivå (`chunkMax` i `src/render/quality.ts`): test 150 chunks (96 000 trianglar), low 100 (64 000), medium 300 (691 200), high 400 (921 600; aldrig över 400 chunks eller 1,5 miljoner trianglar). Den lilla triangeln är priset: en chunkruta är 3 till 7 bildpunkter i nära vyer mot det gamla nätets cirka 80, så fler 2 x 2-rutor skuggas bara till hälften; mät på riktig GPU med `#perf` (terrängraden visar chunks, nivåer, trianglar och om taket stoppade delningar). Efter granskningsfixen (D140, D141) lämnar nya delningar 8 procent av taket ledigt (`CHUNK_BUDGET_RESERVE`): i vyer där taket binder, och bara där, är trianglarna i testnivåns, low-nivåns och high-nivåns kolumner övre gränser (standardvyn och medium-nivån är oförändrade).

Del B (D135): territoriet har inte lagt till några texturhämtningar per bildpunkt; i standardvyn (omloppsbana) hoppar territoriekoden över de sex finare brusoktaverna vid det första testet (en `smoothstep` och en förgrening per bildpunkt med land), så standardnivåns pixelarbete är M05:s plus ett dussin multiplikationer för pixelstorleken (cellerna per bildpunkt från `dFdx(vRel)`); trianglarna är oförändrade jämfört med tabellen ovan (del B ändrar inget i chunk-urvalet). Den första finare oktaven slås på när en bildpunkt är mindre än en tredjedels cell (kamerahöjd under cirka 8 000 km) och alla sex under cirka 400 km (24 heltalshashar till, ungefär 15 procent mer än M05:s territoriepass); där syns få trianglar. Ingen GPU finns här: talen är räkning av instruktioner och texturhämtningar, inte tid.
